Environmental & Occupational Health

Emerging Contaminants
Contaminants of Emerging Concern (CECs) or Emerging Contaminants (ECs), provides an overview of commonly occurring emerging contaminants detected in water, air, food, and other exposure sources, such as household items and personal care products. The lessons discuss where they may be found, how humans and other organisms may be exposed to them, and what potential adverse health effects may occur following exposure. These contaminants can be manufactured or naturally occurring chemicals, microorganisms, or materials not commonly monitored for in the environment, but those that have been recently detected, potentially posing new risks to human health, aquatic life, or other exposed organisms. They often lack published health standards and are characterized by emerging information regarding their environmental presence and harmful impacts, such as endocrine disruption. This course is broken down into two sections, to allow the user time to review and absorb the information, followed by knowledge checks after each section.

Ambient Air Quality
This 2 part course discusses ambient air pollution and emission sources, adverse health effects associated with exposure to air pollution, the Clean Air Act and the 6 criteria pollutants, prescribed burning and Red Cedar trees, air monitoring and Air Quality Index (AQI), and the Nebraska PurpleAir Network.
Teacher: Sue Dempsey; Teacher: Derry Stover

Risk Communication
Risk Communication is a course designed to provide effective strategies when communicating in contentious topics within environmental public health.
Teacher: Sue Dempsey; Teacher: Derry Stover

Fungi Surveillance
This section provides investigation guidelines, case classification flow chart, surveillance process flow chart, and case report forms. The objective is to be able to investigate a histoplasmosis or coccidioidomycosis case from start to finish.
Teacher: Mamie Lush; Teacher: Derry Stover

Lead Poisoning Prevention Local Health Department Training
The Nebraska Lead Poisoning Prevention Program (NeCLPPP) contracted with the National Center for Healthy Housing to develop training modules for DHHS and local health department staff. The modules are for both new and existing staff who work in the lead poisoning prevention area. These modules provide guidelines and recommendations from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and DHHS NeCLPPP Program, information and education from other successful state-based CLPPP Programs, research and reference articles, and highlights key partnerships that are critical for keeping children and families safe from lead. The nine modules provide information for both primary and secondary prevention strategies. The modules also highlight the needs to increase local lead testing for children ages 0-6, ensure effective case management practices are implemented immediately, and to create or enhance referral systems that meets the needs of impacted families. These training modules are each about 30 minutes in length and should take no more than 4 to 5 hours to complete all of them.
Teacher: Brian Coyle; Teacher: Derry Stover

Toxicology 101
The Toxicology 101 course is a 3 part series of videos highlighting basic concepts of toxicology, environmental exposures in soil, air, and water, and human exposure risk assessment. The 3 videos total 36 minutes with a short knowledge check and course evaluation.
Teacher: Sue Dempsey; Teacher: Derry Stover
