=== Slide 2 ===
Project Background
Data Science Problem:
Pathology laboratory may not have an effective filter to avoid non-reportable reports being sent.
 About 1/3 of pathology reports received by the cancer registry were non-reportable.
Certified Tumor Registrar (CTR) needs to manually identify reportability for all reports received.
Project Goal:
Automate the identification process as much as possible 
Align with Data Modernization Initiative (DMI):
Pursue innovation and explore new methods to strengthen data reporting, management, and analytics across public health, as well as to increase efficiency and accuracy

=== Slide 4 ===
Data
Standard electronic pathology reports in HL7 format from 4 pathology laboratories, 4295 reports in total.
Only diagnostic text in OBX-5 was analyzed 
All reports were de-identified 
CTRs in the Nebraska Cancer Registry manually reviewed all reports and filtered out non-reportable reports. Then we compared the resulted and original datasets to encode each report. 
	0 – non-reportable	1 – reportable  

=== Slide 5 ===
Current Solutions
Use case-finding ICD-10 code list:
Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results Program (SEER) case-finding ICD-10 code list: https://seer.cancer.gov/tools/casefinding/
Note: this list includes more codes than those in the reportable list so as not to miss any reportable cases.

Hypothesis: a report is reportable if it contains reportable ICD-10 code(s). 
Include only the reports containing at least one ICD-10 code (3113 reports in total – 72% of all reports).   
Accuracy: 58%

=== Slide 7 ===
NLP Solution
Text preprocess:
Vectorization:
Frequency vector / Count vector
Term frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) vector
Model selection for text classification:
Naïve Bayes 
Logistic Regression
Linear Support Vector Machine
Tokenization
Remove Stop Words
Remove Punctuations
Assign Part-Of-Speech Tags
Lemmatization
A dog ran away.
['A', 'dog', 'ran’, 'away', '.']
['A', 'dog', 'ran', 'away', '.']
['dog', ‘ran', 'away', '.']
['dog', ‘ran', 'away', '.']
['dog', ‘ran', 'away']
('dog', 'NN'), ('ran', 'VBD'), ('away', 'RB’)
         NN: noun, singular 
         VBD: verb, past tense
         RB: adverb 
dog  dog
ran  run
away  away
